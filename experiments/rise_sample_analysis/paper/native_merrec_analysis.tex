\subsection{Conditional benefits relative to the native backbone}
\label{sec:rise_native_merrec_conditions}

\paragraph{A distinct comparison.}
We evaluate Full RISE (E) against a newly completed native HyFormer control (A)
under the matched MerRec seed-20262028 protocol. The native model was trained
without the auxiliary RISE branch; Content-only (B), used in the companion
component analysis, retains auxiliary content projections. Accordingly,
$\delta_g^{E:A}=A_{E,g}-A_{A,g}$ measures the full intervention, whereas
$\delta_g^{E:B}$ measures the additional difference between separately trained
modulation configurations. These estimands should not be substituted for one
another. Both comparisons use the same Full RISE predictions and 11,599,872
retained test rows. All prediction-shard checksums, physical sample identities,
six metadata fields, and five labels were verified. Evaluation follows the
original eight-block incomplete-batch removal, rather than silently adding
the omitted test tails.

The unrounded prediction-based macro ROC-AUC increases from 0.785633637 to
0.800872336, a difference of 0.015238700 with a nominal user-cluster interval
$[0.013878878,0.016598521]$ (Table~\ref{tab:native_global_auc}). All five actions
improve globally, but this does not imply that every subgroup or every quality
metric improves. The comparison concerns this new matched seed, not recovery
of the unavailable original seed-20262027 model instance.

\paragraph{Sample conditions.}
Table~\ref{tab:native_all_conditions} retains all one-dimensional groups.
Their boundaries were fixed before formal effect inspection, with frequency
cut points derived from training counts. Effective length excludes the target
and counts valid positions in a
100-token window. Frequency counts use training targets and global IDs;
positive-frequency entity quartiles are 14/80 for users and 1/4 for items.
Zero frequency means unseen as a training target, without establishing strict
cold start. At fixed length 100, category entropy $H$ and candidate-category
history fraction $Q$ exclude category codes 0 and 309; undefined values remain
separate. Row counts are shared across actions, while positive counts remain
label-specific and are never pooled into a macro count.

The macro gain is 0.019479 at length 100 and 0.012081 at lengths 1--20.
A direct contrast gives 0.007398, with interval $[0.003664,0.011133]$.
Empty history instead yields 0.005278, with interval $[-0.004541,0.015097]$
crossing zero. The positive
Content-only-controlled empty-history result therefore cannot be reused as
native-backbone evidence. Candidate and context inputs remain available, and
separate training and native padding behavior prevent a direct attribution to
history accumulation. The 51--99 point estimate also exceeds that at 100,
so the pattern is not strictly monotone.

Frequency effects are positive in every reported macro group but are not
monotone. Item-frequency gains are 0.011887, 0.016704, 0.017181, and 0.015555
for zero, one, two--four, and at least five occurrences. User-frequency gains
are 0.014254, 0.014794, 0.019759, and 0.019177. These marginal comparisons do
not separate frequency from history length, user composition, or label difficulty.

Fixed-length diversity is likewise nonmonotone: gains across the four defined
entropy groups are 0.004035, 0.022411, 0.014523, and 0.021134. Although the
high-versus-middle entropy contrast is positive, 0.006610
$[0.000914,0.012306]$, this local contrast does not imply increasing gains across
the full entropy range. For sparse nonzero versus high candidate-category
support, the effect difference is only 0.001487 $[-0.006770,0.009744]$.
Thus, the corresponding Content-only-controlled contrast does not establish
the same claim against the native backbone. The three contrasts were selected
after inspecting the initial grouped mean effects of Full RISE minus
Content-only; this native analysis reuses those same definitions. They remain
exploratory, not preregistered or confirmatory, with no multiplicity correction.

\paragraph{Task and probability-quality exceptions.}
The complete subgroup inventory has four negative task-level ROC-AUC point
estimates: Offer and Checkout at empty history, and the same actions in the
near-zero-entropy length-100 group. Every corresponding nominal interval crosses
zero. The latter group has only 24 Offer and four Checkout positives; it also
has just two Purchase positives. Undefined candidate support has no Checkout
or Purchase positives, so its five-action macro AUC is unavailable rather than
recomputed over fewer actions.

We retain original probabilities and supplement ROC-AUC with non-interpolated
average precision (AP), Brier score, and Logloss
(Table~\ref{tab:native_prediction_quality}). Macro AP increases from 0.077907499
to 0.092808705, macro Brier decreases from 0.022998024 to 0.022297116, and
macro Logloss decreases from 0.085562731 to 0.082677531. Nevertheless, Purchase
AP decreases from 0.012851605 to 0.012773943 despite its ROC-AUC improvement.
Offer and Checkout each have small increases in both Brier and Logloss.
These descriptive differences show that ranking gains do not guarantee uniform
probability-quality gains; they have no new confidence intervals. No calibrator
was fitted and no decision threshold was selected. Fixed-grid calibration error
is bin-dependent and is not used as evidence of uniformly improved calibration.

\paragraph{AUC structure and inferential scope.}
For each action, the exact identity
\begin{equation}
 A_m=\sum_{g,h:n_g^+n_h^->0}
 \frac{n_g^+n_h^-}{N^+N^-}A_m^{g,h}
\end{equation}
separates positive--negative pairs within and across the five length groups,
with half credit for ties. Equal-label averaging decomposes the native-control
macro gain into 0.007672426 within-group and 0.007566274 cross-group contributions;
the latter accounts for 49.65\%, not the component comparison's 51.76\%.
The maximum reconstruction residual is $1.74\times10^{-16}$. This is an
algebraic allocation for the chosen partition, not causal attribution.

Intervals aggregate normalized paired AUC influences by user. Group contrasts
retain shared-user covariance, and macro intervals average the five label
influences before estimating variance. They condition on fixed trained models
and do not include seed variability. A user's contribution combines all of
that user's retained observations, including positive and negative examples.
The variance includes the finite-cluster correction; actions sharing users are
not counted as independent replications. The earlier exact-bootstrap check concerned
E versus Content-only; it was not relabeled as an empirical bootstrap validation
of E versus native A. One seed, added model capacity, correlated grouping variables,
HyFormer padding, and unresolved availability times of MerRec's attributed
historical outcomes limit causal and deployment interpretations. The native
comparison should establish the primary conditional performance result; the
component analysis then addresses the separate incremental AM/PA question.
